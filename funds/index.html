State law Oregon Local Budget Law (ORS Ch. 294) defines a fund as a self-balancing accounting entity set aside to carry on a specific activity or to meet certain objectives under a specific regulation. Every Oregon budget has at least one — almost always called the General Fund — for everyday operations; restricted revenues (utility rates, system-development charges, bond proceeds) live in their own funds so they can only be spent on what the law allows. General & supporting funds

The General Fund is the only unrestricted fund. Supporting funds handle revenue streams with restrictions on use.

Transportation

Operating, maintenance, and developer-impact funds for streets and the right-of-way.

Parks

Operating, system-development, and bond-financed funds supporting the parks system.

Utilities (Water · Sewer · Stormwater)

Enterprise-fund pairs — each utility runs an Operating fund (rates & ops) and a Development fund (SDCs for growth-related capital).

Special-purpose & one-time

Bond proceeds, federal grants, internal-service funds, and small dedicated funds.
