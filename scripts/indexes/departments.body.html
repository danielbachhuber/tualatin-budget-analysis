Departments

Every operating department in the FY 2026–27 proposed budget, grouped by program area. Each entry links directly to its section in the source PDF; the deep-dive page tag marks departments with a full breakdown on this site.

Policy & Administration 10.0% of city budget

City Council p. 122
Six councilors plus the mayor. Sets policy direction, adopts the budget, and represents the city to state and federal partners. Operates on stipends and minimal materials.
See breakdown
Administration p. 126
City Manager's office, Deputy City Manager, Assistant City Manager, Human Resources, Volunteer Services, Communications, and economic-development functions.
See breakdown
Finance p. 132
Accounting, financial reporting, budget administration, utility billing, accounts payable and receivable, payroll, and the city's investment portfolio.
See breakdown
Municipal Court p. 137
Adjudicates city ordinance violations, traffic infractions, and certain misdemeanor offenses. Court Administrator, Court Clerks, and contracted judicial services.
See breakdown
Legal p. 142
City Attorney's office. Drafts ordinances and contracts, advises Council and staff, represents the city in litigation, and supports code compliance.
See breakdown
Information Services p. 146
IT infrastructure, cybersecurity, GIS, AV for Council chambers, and applications support across every department.
See breakdown
Non-Departmental p. 152
Citywide overhead not attributable to a single department: insurance pool premiums (CIS), contract lobbying, regional dues, transfers, and reserves.
American Rescue Plan Fund p. 156
Final spend-down of federal ARPA funds. FY 26-27 is the closeout year; the remaining balance is being deployed before the federal deadline.

Community Development 5.4% of city budget

Community Development (Planning) p. 159
Long-range and current planning, land-use applications, the Transportation System Plan update, the Climate Action Plan, the Core Opportunity & Reinvestment Area (CORA), and housing strategy implementation.
See breakdown
Engineering p. 165
Capital project delivery for transportation, water, sewer, and stormwater. Reviews development plans and oversees right-of-way work and Public Works permits.
See breakdown
Building p. 171
Plan review, building inspections, and code compliance under statewide building codes. Operates from a dedicated permit-revenue fund, not the General Fund.
See breakdown

Culture & Recreation 12.4% of city budget

Library p. 177
Public library services, Teen Room, Makerspace, and Summer Reading Program. About 72% of the budget is funded by intergovernmental revenue (WCCLS and Clackamas County Library District).
See breakdown
Parks and Recreation p. 186 See breakdown
Recreation programming, summer camps, Juanita Pohl Center for active aging adults, signature community events (Pumpkin Regatta, Holiday Lights), and parks planning & development. Adding a Recreation Supervisor in FY 26-27.
Parks Maintenance p. 192
Day-to-day maintenance of twelve parks (340 acres), 180 acres of greenway and natural areas, athletic fields, playgrounds, and trails.
See breakdown

Public Safety 10.8% of city budget

Police p. 208
Patrol, investigations, traffic safety (including the photo-enforcement program), school resource officers contracted to Tigard-Tualatin School District, and police services contracted to the City of Durham. The largest single department by personnel-services budget.
See breakdown

Public Works 57.4% of city budget

Public Works contains the city's utility operations and supporting divisions. The high share of the budget reflects capital outlay and reserves carried by the utility funds — see Fund balances for the per-fund breakdown.

Maintenance Services p. 216
Public Works leadership and shared services: facilities maintenance, fleet, equipment shop, and cross-utility coordination.
See breakdown
Water Operating p. 225
Operates 115 miles of pipe, 6 reservoirs (14M gallons), and 1,120 fire hydrants. Buys treated water from the City of Portland. FY 26-27 includes B-Level Reservoir work and the Aquifer Storage and Recovery well.
See breakdown
Sewer Operating p. 236
Sanitary sewer collection. Partners with Clean Water Services for treatment. FY 26-27 includes Martinazzi Avenue and SW 108th sewer upsizing projects.
See breakdown
Stormwater Operating p. 246
Storm drain network and water-quality infrastructure. Catch basin cleaning, regulatory compliance, and improvements to flooding-susceptible areas including Nyberg Creek.
See breakdown
Road Utility Fund p. 255
Pavement Maintenance Program funded by the Road Utility Fee. FY 26-27 is a lower-volume year following last year's high-project cycle.
See breakdown
Road Operating p. 259
Street maintenance, traffic signs and signals (52 signals), and the mag-chloride de-icing program. Maintains 73 miles of streets.
See breakdown
